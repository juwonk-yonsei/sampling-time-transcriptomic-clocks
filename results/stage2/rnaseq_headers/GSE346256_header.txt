!Series_summary	"Immune function follows circadian rhythms, and time-of-day of infusion may influence the efficacy of Chimeric Antigen Receptor T-cell (CAR-T) therapy. To investigate the transcriptional and phenotypic basis of this phenomenon, CD7-CAR T cells and empty vector (EV) controls were synchronized by serum shock and profiled by RNA sequencing across a 24-hour time-course (0, 6, 12, 18, 24 h), alongside cosinor analysis of cytotoxicity, cytokine secretion, exhaustion markers, and immunophenotyping. CD7-CAR T cells showed a more coordinated core clock gene network than EV controls, with hallmark immune/proliferative pathways peaking in a late window (16–20 h) and greater-amplitude oscillations in cytotoxicity and cytokine secretion. Circadian phase inferred in an independent T-ALL cohort (n = 1,334) using CYCLOPS was concordant with the in vitro model, and event-free survival was significantly worse in the fourth phase quartile (p = 0.011). These findings indicate that CD7-CAR T cells retain a partially reorganized circadian oscillator governing their effector programs, supporting infusion timing as a translatable strategy for CAR-T therapy."
